Status key (as in No. 04): **verified** = primary or institutional text read · **corroborated** = two or more
independent secondary sources agree, primary not read · **hedged** = sources disagree or qualify it, and the script
carries that hedge · **RE-CHECK** = re-open before voicing · **not used** = checked and left out.

## Viewer prior vs. claim
- **Prior 1 (the hook):** "Food comes from farms. Soil, sun, rain and a farmer. That's it."
- **Claim that breaks it:** researchers estimate that roughly 40–48 % of people alive are fed thanks to nitrogen
  fertiliser made from the air in factories (Haber–Bosch), and that harvests would be about half without it (F5, F6, F29).
  The episode climbs to that answer rung by rung and plants the word "fertiliser" in 1966.
- **Prior 2 (the guess):** "Food is one of the biggest things I pay for." In 1500 it was 70–80 % of a working family's
  spending (F1). In the US in 2024 it was about 10 % of disposable income, and more than half of that was eating out (F7).

## Myths and disputes, and how the script handles each
| Popular claim | What the evidence says | Script handling |
|---|---|---|
| "Haber–Bosch feeds half the world." | Estimates are ~40 % (Smil, 2000) to ~48 % (Erisman, 2008), both approximate. | Hook "roughly half" → answer "between four and five of every ten" (F5). |
| "Medieval people drank beer because water was unsafe." | Widely disputed by food historians. | **Not used.** |
| "Napoleon offered a prize for preserving food, and Appert won it." | A 1795 prize is often cited, but Appert's 1810 money was an ex gratia government payment, conditional on publishing. | Narrates only the 1810 payment and its condition (F15). |
| "Italians always cooked with tomatoes." / "Tomatoes were thought poisonous everywhere." | Tomatoes reached Italy by 1548; early uses were often ornamental; "poison" stories are mostly English/American and overstated. | Says Mattioli described it in his mandrake chapter and "in some places" it was looked at, not eaten (F11, F12). No "everyone thought it was poison". |
| "The tin can and can opener were invented together." | Opener patents 1855 (UK) / 1858 (US), decades after 1810. | "That comes decades later" (F16). |
| "Takaki discovered vitamins." | He fixed beriberi by changing diet; his protein theory was wrong; vitamins came later (Eijkman, Funk). | "right about the cure, and wrong about the cause" (F20, F21). |
| "Shastri's appeal shut every restaurant on Monday nights." | Weakly sourced. | **Not narrated**; the appeal itself is (F22). |
| "Ultra-processed foods make up 60 %+ of the US diet." | Older NHANES cycles ~57–58 %; latest 2021–23: 55.0 % (age 1+), 53.0 % adults. | "more than half" (F28). |
| "Brazilian scientists invented the word *ultra-processed*." | NOVA (Monteiro et al.) made the concept mainstream; who first wrote the word isn't settled. | "put on the map here" (F27). |

## Candidates checked and NOT used
- **Dunedin frozen-meat ship, NZ 1882** (NZHistory): strong, verified; held as the alternative 1810/preservation era (§12 Q3).
- **Edo "kakke" (beriberi from polished rice, ~1700s Japan):** good, but the Japan era is 1884; mentioning both doubles up.
- **Sweet potato into China, 1594 (Chen Zhenlong):** well known, but the smuggling details are legend-heavy, and 1594 sits
  too close to 1500.
- **Potato in Ireland (labourers eating ~14 lb a day before the Famine):** striking, but it pulls the episode into the
  Famine; better as its own subject.
- **Food systems = 34 % of emissions (Crippa et al., *Nature Food* 2021):** consistent with EAT-Lancet's "nearly 30 %";
  one source is enough.
- **EAT-Lancet "15 million avoidable deaths a year"** (verified, Summary Report p. 9): cut for length; optional (§12).
- **"Agriculture has already converted 37 % of the planet's ice-free land"** (EAT-Lancet 2025, p. 16): verified, unused.
- **Ortiz-Bobea et al. 2021 (−21 % agricultural productivity since 1961):** peer-reviewed, but the IPCC sentence (F31) is
  the more authoritative carrier; no number narrated.
- **IPCC maize-yield maps for 2080–2099 (SPM Fig. SPM.3):** model ranges per warming level; too technical for one line.
- **OWID "48 % in 2023":** an extension of Erisman 2008, not a new estimate.
- **"Twice as many people with obesity as underweight":** my arithmetic across mixed age metrics. Not used.
- **Shastri family "test" and Monday restaurant closures:** single or weak sources.
