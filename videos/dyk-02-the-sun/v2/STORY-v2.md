## 2. Cold open (the strongest 20 s): a concrete paradox
> It's eight o'clock. The Sun's warm on your face. But that light is old. It left the Sun eight minutes and twenty seconds ago.
> So if the Sun vanished right now, you wouldn't know. You'd still see it. You'd still feel it. For eight more minutes.
> Five hundred years ago, the Sun told you when to get up and when to sleep. Today, we turn it into electricity.
> And still, did you know that you have never once seen the Sun as it is... right now?

Why it's stronger than v1's open:
- **The paradox gets a body.** "You'd still *see* it. You'd still *feel* it." Heat is light too (infrared, F6), so the warmth on your face is also 8 min old. v1 stopped at "you would not know".
- **The arc is promised in two sentences** ("told you when to sleep" → "turn it into electricity"). v1's five verbs (celebrated, hidden, split, chased, touched) were a list without a direction.
- It ends on the episode question, in the channel's own words. At the pace targets it runs ~26 s; at v1's measured rate (×0.97) it's ~25 s.

## 4. The answer walks the ladder back (then → now → next)
1. **The light:** 8 min 20 s across 150 million km, so every sunrise you've ever seen was already old (F1).
2. **The guess pays off:** the energy inside it took about 170,000 years to get out of the core (NASA estimate, hedged on screen) and set out before farms, cities and writing (F17). "How close was your guess?" (0.45 s beat).
3. **The ladder:** "Now climb that ladder again, with me. In fifteen hundred, the Sun was day and night. Then something to watch. To explain. To measure. Then it was your lunch. Today, it's power. The Sun didn't change. We did." Each rung lights on its word.
4. **Payoff (v1's proven wording, kept word for word):** "You have never seen the Sun as it is. Only as it was, eight minutes ago."
5. **Next:** su11 opens with "So what's the next rung?", so 2100 grows straight out of the top of the ladder.

## 5. 2100: grown from the top rung, sourced only
- **Evidence 1, internet + AI → energy (A2, IEA *Energy and AI*, 2025):** data centres used ~1.5 % of world electricity in 2024; the IEA expects that to more than double by 2030, "slightly more than Japan's total electricity consumption today". On screen: `IEA projection · uncertainty is high`.
- **Evidence 2, the Sun's share (A1, Ember 2026):** solar made almost 11× more electricity in 2025 than in 2015, about 8.7 % of the world's power (said in the Today era; shown again on the 2100 ladder as the rising line).
- **Evidence 3, orbit (F13):** Caltech MAPLE beamed power wirelessly in space in 2023, a tiny test.
- **Scenario (labelled `PREDICTION · a scenario, not a fact` throughout):** the internet, AI and whole cities running *more and more* on sunlight, some of it *maybe* collected in orbit. The callback line ("In 1500 the Sun decided when you went to sleep. In 2100 it might decide whether your AI wakes up.") is hedged with "might".
- **What I did NOT claim:** no 2100 solar share, no "AI will run on solar", no number past 2030, nothing from the IEA 2014 roadmap (dropped as outdated).
- **Bookend (F15):** "The Sun won't notice. NASA estimates it has another five billion years or so."

## 6. What changed vs v1, and why (honest critique)
**v1 against David's four points**
1. *Human behaviour per century* (v1: **partial**). v1 had behaviour in every era (fires, parasols, sunbathing, indoor life), but it never said *how people saw the Sun*, so the eras read as seven separate trivia stops. 1610 and 1800 were mostly about scientists, not about you. Photosynthesis appeared only as a throwaway clause in the Today era, and the 2100 section never connected to anything the viewer had watched build up. The reveal's recap ("celebrated, hid, split, chased, touched") was a list of verbs, not a ladder.
   → **v2:** one plain "how we saw it" sentence per era, climbing; photosynthesis gets its own rung (1928, named 1893); the answer re-climbs the ladder; 2100 is the next rung (energy behind internet/AI/cities, from IEA data).
2. *Human, friend-like interaction* (v1: **weak**). v1 had almost no contractions ("It is", "you would not know", "you will"). It had essayistic lines ("The perfect heavenly body had blemishes", "The Sun can reach down and touch our machines" were fine; "When that energy began its journey" used an AI-tell word), only one direct question to the viewer before the close, and in the eras it sounded like a narrator reading, not a friend talking.
   → **v2:** contractions throughout (outro ritual lines kept verbatim because the format fixes their wording); direct questions with a beat ("So what is the Sun to you?" · "Why?" · "So what's the Sun to you now?" · "How close was your guess?" · "So what's the next rung?"); one-word and two-word payoffs ("Simple." "Closely." "Tiny. But real." "Power."); sensory openings in every era; no AI-tell words (grep below).
3. *Natural, lively pace* (v1: **passed the gates, but the writing fought the voice**). v1's eras were one 75–101-word chunk each, pushed to speed 1.12–1.18 to fit. That's why the middle sounded rushed and the lean-ins slow.
   → **v2:** every era is split into 5–6 mood chunks (sensory 3.0 · DYK 2.9 lean-in · behaviour 3.0 · what changed 3.0–3.1 · cliffhanger 2.9). No new chunk is over 69 words (v1 had 75–116), so the speeds can sit near 1.0; the reveal is now 4 chunks (light · energy + guess · ladder · payoff). Hook 3.3/3.4, welcome 3.15, outro 3.1–3.2, floors 0.95 there and 0.85 on lean-ins, plus `pauses` blocks for real breaths (0.3 s sentence gaps, 0.2–0.45 s question beats).
4. *Keep what works* (**kept**): format order, Brian only, the guess ("how old is the energy?"), Comment of the Week (EXAMPLE card), Dexty.live, No. 03 · School teaser, approved title and thumbnail, all of v1's verified facts, v1's tested sentence-final fixes ("glowing with firelight", "blackened", "nothing was visible", "as it is today?", "eight minutes ago", "even exist?").

**Against the v1 audit/rubric:** v1 shipped at A 26/30 after 3 audit rounds. Its weakest dimensions were motion (five 2.6–2.9 s stretches carried only by continuous motion) and typography (22 px footnotes). Both are visual. v2's narration gives every era a new beat every 1–2 sentences (questions, chips, one-word payoffs), which leaves fewer of those quiet stretches to cover.

**Concrete content changes**
- 1700s era moves from **1752** to **1779**: Gunning (F4b) stays as the DYK and behaviour beat, and Ingenhousz (A3) adds the "explain" rung. Newton is dropped, since Herschel's prism carries the colours.
- 1928 gains the **photosynthesis rung** (A4); Vanguard 1 is dropped to make room (still in RESEARCH.md).
- Today's DYK changes from Parker (kept as a line) to **solar's ×11 decade** (A1). The Sun as harvested power is the rung.
- 2100 replaces the IEA 2014 "27 % by 2050" vision with **IEA 2025 data-centre electricity** (A2). The future is now tied to what the viewer actually uses: videos and AI.
- v1 reveal "began its journey" (AI-tell word) removed.

## 9. Read-aloud sample (judge the voice on paper)

**Cold open**
It's eight o'clock. The Sun's warm on your face. But that light is old. It left the Sun eight minutes and twenty seconds ago.
So if the Sun vanished right now... you wouldn't know. You'd still see it. You'd still feel it. For eight more minutes.
Five hundred years ago, the Sun told you when to get up and when to sleep. Today, we turn it into electricity.
And still, did you know that you have never once seen the Sun as it is... right now?

**1500**
You wake up in fifteen hundred, in London. It's Midsummer Eve. You can smell wood smoke, and the streets are glowing with firelight.
Did you know that almost everyone you'll ever meet believes the Sun goes around the Earth? That's what Ptolemy taught, about fourteen hundred years ago.
So what is the Sun to you? ... Simple. It's the difference between day and night. Daylight is for work. Darkness is for sleep, and prayer.
But tonight, nobody sleeps. Doors are hung with green birch and flowers. Glass lamps burn all night. Neighbours share bread and drink by the bonfires. Why? ... A London writer, John Stow, later wrote that a great fire could purge the infection of the air.
Far away, a young Polish astronomer, Copernicus, is studying the sky. In fifteen forty three, the year he dies, his book puts the Sun at the centre.
And within seventy years, a new instrument will show you something shocking. The Sun isn't perfect.

**The answer**
Here's the answer. Light is fast. But it isn't instant. It takes about eight minutes and twenty seconds to cross a hundred and fifty million kilometres. Every sunrise you've ever watched was already old.
And the energy inside it? ... It was made deep in the Sun's core, and it spent a very long time struggling to get out. NASA estimates about a hundred and seventy thousand years. When it set out, nobody on Earth had farms. Or cities. Or writing. How close was your guess? ...
Now climb that ladder again, with me. In fifteen hundred, the Sun was day and night. Then something to watch. To explain. To measure. Then it was your lunch. Today, it's power. The Sun didn't change. We did. ...
You have never seen the Sun as it is. Only as it was, eight minutes ago.
