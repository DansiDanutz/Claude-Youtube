## Candidates checked and NOT used
- **"Sunlight hitting Earth in one hour (or 1.5 h) equals humanity's yearly energy use."** Popular, but the figure depends on top-of-atmosphere vs surface irradiance and the year; a university calculation stretches it to ~5.5 h with realistic surface irradiance. Contested → not used.
- **Statute of Artificers 1563 (5 Eliz. I c. 4) hours clause** ("from five of the clock in the morning…", "from the spring of the day… until the night"). I could not reach the statute text to verify the wording → not used. It would be a strong 1500-era behaviour fact if David wants it verified from *Statutes of the Realm*.
- **IEA 2014 solar roadmap (27 % by 2050)** (v1 F14): still true as a historical vision, but dropped in v2: it is 12 years old, and newer measured data (A1) plus the IEA's 2025 data-centre projection (A2) tell the 2100 story better.
- **"Solar is the fastest-growing source"** as a general claim: Ember says 2025's rise in solar was the largest of any source ever apart from coal's 2021 rebound. That is too fiddly to say cleanly → not narrated.
- **IEA 2035 data-centre range (≈ 1,200 TWh base, higher in some cases)**: scenario-dependent; kept out of the narration to avoid stacking projections.
