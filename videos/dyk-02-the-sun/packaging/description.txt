It is eight o'clock in the morning and the Sun is warm on your face. But that light left the Sun 8 minutes and 20 seconds ago. If the Sun vanished right now, you would not know.

And in 2100? We follow the published evidence (a Caltech satellite that beamed power in space, the IEA's solar roadmaps) to one scenario for how we could live with the Sun, clearly labelled as a prediction, not a fact.

Wake up seven times (1500, 1610, 1752, 1800, 1928, today and 2100) and see how people really behaved toward the Sun: Midsummer bonfires in Tudor London, the first telescope drawing of sunspots, white-lead face paint and parasols, Herschel's invisible light, the suntan myth, the Parker Solar Probe. Then the answer: how old the energy in that light really is.
