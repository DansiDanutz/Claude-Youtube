# Did You Know That? No. 02 — "The Sun" · STORY (treatment for approval — no narration generated yet)

**Subject:** the Sun — how humans changed their behaviour toward it, 1500 → today → 2100.
**Episode question (hook):** "Every time you look at the Sun, you are looking into the past. Did you know that
the Sun you see is not the Sun as it is right now?" *(promised in No. 01's outro — paid off here)*
**Viewer prior → violated:** "The Sun is just there — light by day, gone at night." → we went from fearing it,
to measuring it, to wearing it, to flying toward it — and the light hitting your eyes left it 8 min 20 s ago.

## Cold open (frame 0)
A sunrise. A clock reads 08:00:00. The Sun on screen is labelled **"08:00 — but this light left the Sun at 07:51:40."**
"If the Sun vanished right now, you would not know for eight minutes and twenty seconds." (F1)

## Eras (five beats each: wake up · did you know that · how people lived · what changed · cliffhanger)
| Era | Wake up | Did you know that… | How people lived (visual) | What changed | Cliffhanger |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1500** | A field at dawn; the day starts with the Sun, not a clock | Almost everyone you know believes the Sun travels around the Earth (Ptolemy's model, taught for 1,400 years) | Day Clock: sunrise → sunset work, sleep after dark | 1543: Copernicus puts the Sun at the centre (*De revolutionibus*, printed in his final months) (F2) | "Seventy years later, a man points a new instrument at the Sun — and sees it is not perfect." |
| **1610s** | Padua; a forbidden view | The Sun has **spots** — and they move (Galileo, *Letters on Sunspots*, 1613; Harriot sketched them in 1610) (F3) | The Sun as a flawless heavenly body vs. a blemished star | Telescope → the Sun becomes something you can *measure* | "A century later, one prism will split its light open." |
| **1700s** | A fine lady under a parasol | Pale skin was a sign of wealth — a tan meant you worked outside (F4) | Week Strip: indoors for the rich, fields for the poor | 1704: Newton's *Opticks* — white sunlight is all colours (F5) | "And beyond the red… there is light no one can see." |
| **1800s** | An astronomer with thermometers | Herschel (1800) found invisible light beyond red — infrared — with a prism and blackened thermometers (F6) | Day Clock lit by gas lamps — night is being pushed back | 1839: Becquerel, 19, makes sunlight produce electricity (F7) · 1859 Carrington flare (callback to our Carrington film) | "It would take a century to turn that spark into power." |
| **1900s** | A beach in the 1920s | The famous "Coco Chanel invented the suntan" story is probably a myth — historians trace it to a 1971 magazine article; the tan became fashionable as work moved indoors (F8) | Week Strip with the new weekend at the beach | 1954: Bell Labs' silicon cell, ~6 % efficient (F9) · 1958 Vanguard 1 runs on sunlight · 1969: humans on the Moon | "Today, we did something no generation before us could: we touched it." |
| **Today** | You wake up; your phone charged overnight, maybe by solar | Solar is now, for the best projects, "the cheapest electricity in history" (IEA, 2020) (F10) · Dec 2024: Parker Solar Probe passed 6.1 million km from the Sun, the fastest human-made object (F11) | Day Clock: sunscreen, screens indoors, vitamin-D | Every plant, every meal is stored sunlight — photosynthesis (F12) | "So what happens when we stop waiting for the Sun to rise?" |
| **2100 · PREDICTION** | (evidence only) | 2023: Caltech's MAPLE beamed power wirelessly in space and aimed a signal at Earth (F13) · IEA roadmaps once set solar as a possible #1 electricity source by 2050 — a target, not a forecast (F14) | Scenario: power collected in orbit; cities designed for sunlight | The Sun has ~5 billion years left (F15) | → the answer |

## The answer (reveal)
We spent five centuries changing how we *behave* toward the Sun — fear, measure, avoid, worship, harvest —
but it never changed for us. And the light you saw this morning left it before you finished waking up.
**"You have never seen the Sun as it is. Only as it was, eight minutes ago."**
